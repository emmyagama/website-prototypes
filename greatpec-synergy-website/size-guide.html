Two numbers that matter

Watts are not watt-hours.

A power station needs enough output for appliances running at the same time and enough stored energy for the hours you need it.

Output in watts (W)

Think of watts as how much power the station can deliver at once. Add the appliances you plan to run together and check for a fridge or tool startup surge.

Example: A 600W station cannot run a set of appliances that needs more than its rated output, even if the battery has plenty of stored energy.

Capacity in watt-hours (Wh)

Think of watt-hours as the size of the energy tank. More Wh can mean longer runtime, but the real result depends on appliance draw, inverter losses and the way the unit is used.
